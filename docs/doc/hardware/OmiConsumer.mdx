## Overview

The Omi Consumer Version 1 (CV1) is the production-ready Omi hardware designed for everyday use. It features significant upgrades over the DevKit series.

## Firmware

The recommended way to update your device is through the Omi app's built-in OTA (Over-The-Air) update feature.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Install the Omi App">
    Download from the [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/friend-ai-wearable/id6502156163) or [Google Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.friend.ios)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect Your Device">
    Pair your Omi via Bluetooth
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check for Updates">
    The app will notify you when firmware updates are available
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Recover an unresponsive CV1

The CV1 mainboard has a hardware-assisted MCU reset for cases where the device
has power but is not advertising or responding to the app. This is a reboot,
not a factory reset, and it does not intentionally erase stored recordings.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Remove the CV1 from its charger">
    Start with the pendant completely off the magnetic charging puck.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hold the center button">
    Press and keep holding the pendant's center button.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Place it on the powered charger">
    While the button is already held, place the CV1 on the powered magnetic
    puck. Keep holding for about two seconds, then release.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reconnect in the Omi app">
    Allow the device to boot and reconnect. If it does not reconnect, toggle
    Bluetooth in the app's phone and repeat the reset once.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
The two light sources visible beside the center switch are duplicate RGB LEDs
wired to the same red, green, and blue signals. Both showing red is one device
status, not two independent fault codes.
</Info>

<Warning>
Do not short exposed test pads, bridge battery contacts, or pull the internal
flex cable to reset the device. The charger-button sequence is the designed
recovery path.
</Warning>

### Why an older CV1 may turn itself back on

Historical CV1 firmware used the center button for both shutdown and wake. It
could enter system-off while the active-low button was still held, after already
arming that same level as the wake source. The remaining part of the shutdown
press could therefore wake the device immediately and make it appear to reboot
on its own. This was a firmware race, not evidence of two independent LED fault
codes or necessarily a damaged device.

Firmware containing the release fence waits until the physical button is no
longer pressed before arming it as the system-off wake source. A different
lookalike is also possible on storage-protecting builds: if pending audio cannot
be committed safely, shutdown is cancelled and the device deliberately returns
to operation rather than discarding the recording.

On firmware versions with emergency button recovery, a normal 3-second hold
requests a data-safe power-off; release after its feedback. If the device
returns to red instead of staying off, hold continuously for 30 seconds to
request a cold reboot. This does not replace the charger-button reset when the
firmware itself is unresponsive.
